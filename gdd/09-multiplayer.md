# Multiplayer, parties and pets

## Decided

- 2 to 20 players share the dungeon. Players start alone.
- **Parties hold 6**, counting real players and NPC companions. Players can group up if they find each other and
  both wish to join.
- **Crawlers must be clearly marked.** Players and their NPC teammates need clear indicators that show they are
  crawlers and not monsters.
- **Warnings:** enemies that are much higher level than you carry a warning, and so do elite enemies.
- **Player versus player is allowed.** Killing a player gives the killer a **player-killer skull that can never be
  removed**.
- **Pets are not guaranteed.** Keeping a pet is an ability. By default you can adopt a **single pet**, and only
  if you find one through exploration or as a quest reward. Pet-based classes can grow the number of pet slots.
- When a player's crawler dies, they may take over one of their living NPC party members (see
  `02-crawler-and-death.md`).

## Proposed

| Rule | Proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Crawler marker | A coloured ring under the feet and a name plate: one colour for real players, another for NPC crawlers, with your own party in a third |
| Attitude colours | Name plates are green for friendly, yellow for neutral, red for aggressive |
| Much-higher-level warning | A skull by the name when an enemy is 5 or more levels above you |
| Elite marker | A distinct border on the name plate and a glow on the sprite, plus the System AI's comment the first time you meet one |
| The skull | Shown on the name plate to everyone. Factions, shops and the System AI react to it; other players get a reward for killing a skull-bearer |
| Where PvP is off | Safe rooms and stairwells |
| Pets | Pets level up, can die, and count toward the party of 6 |
| NPC companions | Recruited in the world, follow simple orders (attack, hold, follow), and have their own class and gear |
